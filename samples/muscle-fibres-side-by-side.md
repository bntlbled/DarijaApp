# Lyaf l3adalat (muscle fibres): English · Moroccan · Algerian

Side-by-side version of [muscle-fibres-darija.md](muscle-fibres-darija.md). Darija is in Arabizi (3 = ع, 7 = ح, 9 = ق, gh = غ, kh = خ, ch = ش). Each row is the same paragraph in all three.

**Judgement calls (short notes)**
- *Muscle / fibre*: both versions use **l3adala / l3adalat** for muscle and **lif / lyaf** for fibre. In everyday speech many people simply say *muscle* and *fibre* in French, which is also fine.
- *Weightlifter*: Moroccan uses **li kayrfa3 l7did** ("who lifts iron"); Algerian uses **li yrfed l7did** (*rfed* is the usual Algerian verb for "lift"). Gym talk in both countries often just says *musculation*.
- *Capillaries*: rendered as **l3rou9 r-r9a9** (thin veins/vessels) with *capillaires* in brackets, since there is no common everyday word.
- *Qaf*: Moroccan is written with **9**. Many Algerians pronounce qaf as **g** (e.g. *gal*, *gdem*), but **9** is kept in the Algerian text so it reads across regions.
- *Present tense*: Moroccan uses the **ka-** prefix (*kayjri*); Algerian normally has no prefix (*yjri*) and uses **rah** for "is (now)".
- *Third question*: your original message did not show the third question (only the answer), so its heading is our best guess in brackets.
- *Fibroid*: both use the French **fibrome** with *r-r7em* (uterus), as that is what people say at the doctor's.

| The fibre-type mix is also different. Much of that split is inherited, so the sport selects for it as much as training creates it. | **Capillaries.** In a runner, each fibre is surrounded by more capillaries, and because the fibres are small, the number of capillaries per square millimetre is much higher. Lifters keep a reasonable number of capillaries per fibre, but each fibre is so large that capillary density per area drops. | **Mitochondria and myoglobin.** Endurance fibres are full of mitochondria and myoglobin, the oxygen-storing protein that gives “red” muscle its colour. | **Myofibrils.** In a lifter, most of the extra fibre volume is myofibrils, the actin and myosin bundles that produce force. That is why force per fibre goes up. Hadi 3lach l9owwa f kol lif tzid. Kayen chwiya ta3 niqach wach t-tadrib t-t9il ykabber tani l-ajza2 ta3 l-ma w l-ajza2 li ma t9bedch f l-lif (sarcoplasmic hypertrophy), bassah d-dala2il mkhalta. |
| In the literal physical sense of mass per volume, the two are almost the same. Muscle tissue is about 1.06 g/cm³ whichever way it’s trained. What changes is what that volume is used for: a runner devotes more of it to oxygen supply and energy production, and a lifter devotes more of it to contractile machinery. | B l-ma3na l-fiziya2i dyal l-kutla f l-7ejm, j-joj ta9riban kifkif. | **Can type 2 switch to type 1 and vice versa** | **Wach type 2 ymken yt7wwel l type 1 w l3aks?** | **Wach type 2 ynajem yetbeddel l type 1 w l3aks?** |
| Yes, but how far a fibre can shift depends on the direction and on what’s driving the change. Fibre type isn’t fixed at birth. A fibre’s type is set by which myosin heavy chain it makes, and that depends heavily on the motor nerve supplying it and how that nerve fires. Fibre types are best thought of as a continuum, I ↔ I/IIa ↔ IIa ↔ IIa/IIx ↔ IIx, with “hybrid” fibres in between that make two myosin types at once. | Ah, walakin 9ed ach ymken l-lif yt7wwel kayt3alla9 b l-ittijah w b chno li kaydfe3 t-tbdil. Nou3 l-lif machi tabet mn l-wilada. Nou3 l-lif kayt7dded b l-myosin heavy chain li kaysna3, w hadchi kayt3alla9 bzzaf b l3sab l-7araki (motor nerve) li kaywsslo w kifach had l3sab kaykhdem. 7sen tchouf anwa3 l-lyaf b7al silsila mtwasla, I ↔ I/IIa ↔ IIa ↔ IIa/IIx ↔ IIx, w f l-wast kaynin lyaf "hybrides" li kaysn3o joj anwa3 dyal myosin f nefs l-wa9t. | Ih, bassah 9addach ynajem l-lif yetbeddel yet3alla9 b l-ittijah w b wach li ydez3 t-tbdil. Nou3 l-lif machi tabet mn l-wilada. Nou3 l-lif yet7added b l-myosin heavy chain li ysena3, w hadi tet3alla9 bezzaf b l3asab l-7araki (motor nerve) li ywasslo w kifach yakhdem had l3asab. Khir tchouf anwa3 l-lyaf kima silsila mtwasla, I ↔ I/IIa ↔ IIa ↔ IIa/IIx ↔ IIx, w f l-wast kaynin lyaf "hybrides" ysena3ou zouj anwa3 ta3 myosin f nefs l-wa9t. |
| **Within type II (IIx ↔ IIa).** This is the easy, common shift. Almost any regular training, whether endurance, strength or sprint, moves IIx fibres toward IIa within weeks. A trained person ends up with very few pure IIx fibres. Stopping training reverses it: IIx comes back, and after a period of heavy training followed by detraining, IIx can briefly overshoot the person’s original level. | **Wast type II (IIx ↔ IIa).** Hada howa t-tbdil s-sahel w l-m3rouf. Ta9riban ay tadrib mntadam, sawa2 endurance, l9owa wla sprint, kaynaqel l-lyaf IIx l IIa f chi simanat. Bnadem m3llem (trained) kaywlli 3ndo 9lil bzzaf dyal l-lyaf IIx s-safyin. Ila w9ef t-tadrib kayrja3 kolchi: IIx kayrja3, w b3d wa7d l-modda dyal tadrib t9il mn b3d w9ef (detraining), IIx ymken yfout chwiya l-mustawa l-asli dyalo l modda 9sira. | **Wast type II (IIx ↔ IIa).** Hada howa t-tbdil s-sahel w l-ma3rouf. Ta9riban ay tadrib mnadham, sawa2 endurance, l9owwa wella sprint, ynaqel l-lyaf IIx l IIa f chwiya ta3 semanat. L-wa7ed li mdarreb (trained) yweli 3ando chwiya bezzaf ta3 l-lyaf IIx s-safyin. Ila 7bes t-tadrib yerja3 kolech: IIx yerja3, w ba3d modda ta3 tadrib t9il mn ba3d 7bes (detraining), IIx ynajem yfout chwiya l-mustawa l-asli nta3o l modda 9sira. |
| **Type II → type I.** This is possible but limited in people. Years of endurance training appear to raise the share of type I fibres modestly, often through hybrid I/IIa fibres along the way, but there’s little evidence that a sprinter-type muscle can be trained into a marathoner-type muscle. Animal experiments show that complete conversion is biologically possible: when researchers stimulate a fast muscle’s nerve continuously with a slow, low-frequency pattern for weeks, the muscle becomes almost entirely slow. Ordinary training never sends signals that constant or extreme. | **Type II → type I.** Hadchi mumkin walakin m7dod 3nd bnadem. Snin dyal tadrib t-ta7ammul kayban bli kayzido chwiya f n-nisba dyal type I, ghaliban mrorran b lyaf hybrides I/IIa, walakin ma kaynach dala2il bzzaf bli l3adala dyal sprinter ymken tdrrbha w twlli l3adala dyal marathonien. T-tajarib 3la l-7ayawanat katbayen bli t-t7wil l-kamel mumkin biologiyan: mnin l-bahitin kayn3asho l3sab dyal l3adala sri3a b-chkel mustamir b pattern bti2 w b fréquence nazla l simanat, l3adala katwlli bti2a ta9riban kollha. T-tadrib l-3adi 3mmro ma kaysifet ichārat b had l-istimrar wla b had t-tatarruf. | **Type II → type I.** Hadi mumkina bassah m7douda 3and bnadem. Snin ta3 tadrib t-ta7ammul yban bli yzidou chwiya f n-nisba ta3 type I, ghaliban yfoutou b lyaf hybrides I/IIa, bassah ma kanch dala2il bezzaf bli l3adala ta3 sprinter tnajem tdarrebha w tweli l3adala ta3 marathonien. T-tajarib 3la l-7ayawanat tbayen bli t-t7wil l-kamel mumkin biologiyan: ki l-bahithin yna3chou l3asab ta3 l3adala sri3a b chkel mustamir b pattern bti2 w b fréquence nazla l semanat, l3adala tweli bti2a ta9riban kamel. T-tadrib l-3adi 3omro ma yba3at icharat b had l-istimrar wella b had t-tatarruf. |
| **Type I → type II.** The main cause is disuse rather than training. Bed rest, limb immobilisation, spinal cord injury and spaceflight all push fibres toward the fast end, because the steady, low-level activity that keeps type I fibres “slow” disappears. Heavy strength or sprint training does not reliably turn type I fibres into type II. Those fibres mostly just get bigger. | **Type I → type II.** S-sabab l-ra2isi howa 3adam l-isti3mal machi t-tadrib. L-b9a f l-frach, t-tajbir (immobilisation) dyal chi tarf, jar7a f n-nkha3 ach-chawki (moelle épinière) w s-safar l l-fada2, kolhom kaydfe3o l-lyaf l j-jiha s-sri3a, 7it n-nachat l-mustamir w l-khfif li kaykhalli type I "bti2" kaytla7. T-tadrib t-t9il dyal l9owa wla sprint ma kayt7wwelch type I l type II b chkel mdmoun. Dok l-lyaf ghaliban ghir kaykbaro. | **Type I → type II.** S-sabab l-ra2isi howa 3adam l-isti3mal machi t-tadrib. L-b9a f l-frach, t-tajbir (immobilisation) ta3 tarf mn l-jesm, jar7a f n-nkha3 ach-chawki (moelle épinière) w s-safar l l-fada2, ga3 ydez3ou l-lyaf l j-jiha s-sri3a, 3la khater n-nachat l-mustamir w l-khfif li ykhalli type I "bti2" yrou7. T-tadrib t-t9il ta3 l9owwa wella sprint ma ybeddelch type I l type II b chkel madmoun. Hadouk l-lyaf ghaliban ghir ykebrou. |
| **The nerve controls it.** Classic cross-reinnervation experiments in the 1960s connected a slow muscle to the nerve of a fast muscle and the other way round. Each muscle then took on the properties of its new nerve, which showed that the activity pattern from the nerve largely sets fibre type. | **L3sab howa li kaytkem.** F s-sittinat, tajarib klassikiya dyal cross-reinnervation rbto l3adala bti2a b l3sab dyal l3adala sri3a w l3aks. Kol 3adala khdat l-khasa2is dyal l3sab j-jdid dyalha, w hadchi bayyen bli pattern dyal n-nachat li jay mn l3sab howa li kayt7dded bih ktar nou3 l-lif. | **L3asab howa li yetkhakkem.** F s-sittinat, tajarib klassikiya ta3 cross-reinnervation rabtou 3adala bti2a b l3asab ta3 3adala sri3a w l3aks. | **Ageing.** Older muscle tends to look more type I. This is mostly because type II fibres shrink and some are lost along with their motor neurons, and surviving fibres are often “adopted” by slow motor neurons, rather than because of a straightforward switch. | **L-kbor f s-sen.** L3adala dyal li kber f s-sen kattban ktar type I. Hadchi aslan 7it l-lyaf dyal type II kaysghar w chi wa7din menhom kaymchiw m3a l-motor neurons dyalhom, w l-lyaf li b9aw ghaliban katbennahom motor neurons bti2in, machi 7it kayn tbdil mubachir. | **L-kbor f s-sen.** L3adala ta3 l-kbar f s-sen tban ktar type I. Hadi asasan 3la khater l-lyaf ta3 type II yesgharou w chwiya menhom yrou7ou m3a l-motor neurons nta3hom, w l-lyaf li b9aw ghaliban ytbennawhom motor neurons bti2in, machi 3la khater kayen tbdil mubachir. |
| So in practice, training shifts fibres mostly within the type II group, any II → I change from endurance work is modest, and a large slow-to-fast change usually comes from inactivity rather than from lifting. | Ya3ni f l-wa9i3, t-tadrib kaynaqel l-lyaf ktar wast l-groupe dyal type II, ay tbdil mn II → I b sabab l-endurance kaykoun sghir, w t-tbdil l-kbir mn l-bti2 l s-sri3 ghaliban kayji mn 9illat l-7araka machi mn rf3 l7did. | Ya3ni f l-wa9i3, t-tadrib ynaqel l-lyaf ktar wast l-groupe ta3 type II, ay tbdil mn II → I b sabab l-endurance ykoun sghir, w t-tbdil l-kbir mn l-bti2 l s-sri3 ghaliban yji mn 9illat l-7araka machi mn rfed l7did. |

| **(Should I aim to have more type II fibres as I age?)** | **(Wach khassni nkhddem bach ykounu 3ndi lyaf type II ktar mnin nkbar?)** | **(Wach lazem nkhdem bach ykounou 3andi lyaf type II ktar ki nekber?)** |

| **Type II fibres are what ageing takes first.** From about middle age, type II fibres shrink much more than type I fibres, and some are lost when their motor neurons die. That’s a big part of why power (how quickly you can produce force) declines faster than strength with age, and power is what you need to get up from a chair, climb stairs or catch yourself when you trip. Grip strength and leg power are consistently linked to independence, fall risk and mortality in older adults. | Smooth muscle cells are quite different from the fibres we’ve been discussing. | A fibroid starts from a single smooth muscle cell in the myometrium, the muscular wall of the uterus. That cell multiplies, so each fibroid is essentially a clone.
